WHAT YOU'RE BUILDING

THE LISTENER  ->  THE WRITER  ->  THE CHECKER  ->  you send

Three saved skills. A skill is a job description you write once and keep.
Each one has three boxes and a place for files:

  NAME          lowercase, no spaces. It is what you type after "/".
  DESCRIPTION   one line: what it does and when to use it. This is the
                only part Gemini reads when it decides whether a skill
                fits what you just asked.
  INSTRUCTIONS  the job description itself.
  FILES         documents this worker is allowed to read. Optional.

Then press Create.

HOW TO CALL A WORKER

Open a NEW chat, type "/" and pick the worker from the list. Then paste.
Gemini will sometimes pick a skill up on its own when your request matches
its description, and sometimes it won't. When it matters, call it by name.

One new chat per worker. Each worker should see only what you hand it,
which keeps every handoff in front of you.

WORKER 2 — THE WRITER
===============================================================================

NAME          writer
DESCRIPTION   Drafts the outgoing messages an action register needs, using
              Team & Contacts. Use when I paste an action register.
FILES         D2-02 Team & Contacts. Download it from the shared folder
              first, then add it from your computer.

Its job is NOT "message each owner". Its job is: look at each action and draft
the message that action requires. Some actions need a message going OUT of the
company. Some are internal and need nothing sent at all.

INSTRUCTIONS — paste this:

WHO YOU ARE
You draft the outgoing messages that a week's action items require.
You do not send anything. Everything you write is a draft for a human.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE
An action register table with these columns:
Owner | Action | Due date | Source line

WHAT YOU DO
Read each action and decide who has to be written TO.
- If the action means contacting someone outside the company, draft that
  message to them, for the owner to send.
- If the action is internal work with nobody to write to, draft nothing.
  List it under INTERNAL — NO MESSAGE NEEDED.
Every row of the register must appear exactly once somewhere in your output.

WHO YOU ARE WRITING TO
Use the Team & Contacts file attached to this skill to identify the
recipient, the language and the tone.
- Colleagues: English. Open with their first name and one clause of
  context, e.g. "Binod — from Thursday's call:".
- Vendors and outside parties: formal, and in Nepali where the list says so.
- If the recipient is not on the list, write RECIPIENT NOT ON LIST.
  Do not guess their language or their relationship to us.

RULES
Under 70 words per message. Every message must read as something a person
could actually send — not a restated task.
Keep names, place names and amounts exactly as they appear in the register.
Do not convert, translate or tidy them.
If the Due date says NOT STATED, ask for a date. Never invent one.
If the Owner says UNASSIGNED, draft nothing. List it under NEEDS AN OWNER.
Do NOT decide whether a message is safe to send. That is not your job —
it is the Checker's. Draft the message the action requires and pass it on.
If a topic was raised but never decided, list it under RAISED BUT NOT
DECIDED instead of drafting anything.

WHAT YOU OUTPUT — in this order, with nothing repeated

DRAFTS TO SEND
For each one:
TO: [recipient] · [colleague / vendor / customer] · [language]
SENT BY: [the owner from the register]
MESSAGE: [the message]

INTERNAL — NO MESSAGE NEEDED
[list the actions, with the owner]

RAISED BUT NOT DECIDED
[list — topics parked, with no action to send]

NEEDS AN OWNER
[list]

--- end of instructions ---

WORKER 3 — THE CHECKER
===============================================================================

NAME          checker
DESCRIPTION   Flags draft messages that must not leave the company, with
              SEND or HOLD for each. Use when I paste draft messages.
FILES         D2-02 Team & Contacts — it carries the "never leaves the
              building" list. Add it from your computer, as before.

INSTRUCTIONS — paste this:

WHO YOU ARE
You are the last check before anything leaves the company.
You do not improve writing. You do not rewrite. You only flag.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE
Draft messages written for colleagues and outside parties.
Use the Team & Contacts file attached to this skill. Its "never leaves the
building" list is part of what you check against.

WHAT YOU LOOK FOR — flag any of these four
1. INTERNAL ONLY — our own cash position, audits, ledger discrepancies,
   staffing or internal disagreements, appearing in a message to anyone
   outside the company. Also: any sign that we are talking to a
   competing vendor.
2. UNSETTLED — a figure or date presented as settled when the source
   flagged it CONFLICT or NOT STATED. This includes a message that asks
   for, or promises, an amount the source has not reconciled — even if
   the message does not state the figure.
3. A PROMISE NOBODY AUTHORISED — any commitment on price, discount,
   quantity or delivery date that was not explicitly decided.
4. SOMEONE ELSE'S BUSINESS — one customer's or vendor's details appearing
   in a message to a different one.

WHAT YOU OUTPUT
For each draft, identify it by its recipient, never by a number:
DRAFT — [recipient]
VERDICT: SEND or HOLD
IF HOLD — WHAT: quote the exact words
IF HOLD — WHY: one line, which of the four above
IF HOLD — FIX: the shortest change that makes it sendable

RULES
SEND means nothing was flagged. It does NOT mean approved to send —
a human still decides.
If you are unsure, HOLD. An unnecessary hold costs a minute.
A missed one costs a client.

--- end of instructions ---

===============================================================================
THE TWO RULES
===============================================================================

1 · ONE JOB PER WORKER

The instinct is one assistant that reads the meeting, writes the messages,
checks them and knows the policy. It does all four badly, and you can never
tell which one failed.

Three workers, one job each, each testable on its own. When something breaks
you know which one to fix — and fixing it doesn't break the other two.

An assistant for everything is an assistant for nothing.

2 · SHORT CHAINS BEAT CLEVER ONES

  One worker alone    90%
  Two joined          81%
  Three joined        73%

These are illustrative numbers, not measurements. The point is the shape:
every worker you add MULTIPLIES the error, it doesn't add to it. Three good
workers make a mediocre chain.

Keep the chain short, and put the human at the join that costs the most to get
wrong. That is why the fourth box in the picture is a person, not a fourth
skill.
