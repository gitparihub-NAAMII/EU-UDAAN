# Course 3 — Responsible AI

*Course capability: Responsible AI Governance · Framework: **TRUST** · 26 sub-units*

**Maya entering:** everything runs flawlessly, and she can't vouch for it. **Maya leaving:** she can
stand behind what the machines do in her name.

Course 2 made Maya's department the most reliable in the company, so nobody questions the agents.
Course 3 asks whether what they do is *right* or merely error-free: a chatbot inventing a refund
policy (anchored on the real Air Canada case), two identical CVs scored differently, customer data
pasted into a public tool, and work sent out with nobody's name on it.

**What the learner leaves with:** an audit sampling habit, a classify-before-paste routine, and a
one-page personal AI policy, which they then turn on something they have already built.

| Letter | Meaning | Earned in |
|---|---|---|
| **T** — Trace | Track every claim to a source; sample, quote, keep a trail | Unit 2 |
| **R** — Respect | Protect the data and the people it belongs to | Unit 4 |
| **U** — Uncover | Hunt the bias you can't see; swap-test, keep a human deciding | Unit 3 |
| **S** — Show | Be open that AI was involved, where it matters | Unit 5 |
| **T** — Take | Take the accountability; a named human signs | Unit 5 |

> **The bright line,** stated twice in this course (1.5 and 3.4): *for a decision about a person,
> AI recommends and a named human decides.*
