# Health — Checking a health claim before it is shared or answered

*Sector course · problem bank **H4** · **The Drill way** · Course capability: checking a
forwarded health claim with AI before sharing or replying, and answering it so that a worried
person can act · Framework candidate: SHARE (provisional) · 22 sub-units · **Status: skeleton,
draft. The bank review rates this problem's evidence low ("check first"); confirm with
practitioners before building further***

One of the five health courses, each on one health problem and each taught a different way:
[`3-course-flow/health-programme.md`](../../3-course-flow/health-programme.md). This one is
drilled: a station against the clock, run cold; the answer sheet; a debrief; two more stations
with the check; a card for the deck ([`five-ways.md`](../../1-how-we-built-it/five-ways.md) §3.3).

**Sunil entering:** forwards arrive all day, he pastes each one into a free AI tool, asks "is this
true?", and shares or replies on the verdict. **Sunil leaving:** before anything is shared or
answered he has found and opened the source behind the verdict, written the reply for the
person who is scared, read what the tool read from any screenshot and checked it against the
official channel, and handed anything about the hospital itself to a named person.

**Learner's complaint:** *"I checked it with AI. So why did I share something false?"*

Sunil is the information officer at the 100-bed private hospital in Nepalgunj, the hospital
where Bikash works the insurance desk. He runs its Facebook page and the phone number on the
poster, and the forwards land on both: a kitchen remedy, a claim with three references, a
worried parent, a screenshot of a notice, and a post about the hospital itself. Each is a
station. Each goes wrong a different way.

**Learners:** hospital information and public relations staff, health office communication
focal persons, FCHV supervisors, and anyone who answers the public for a facility.

**What the learner leaves with:** a deck of five reflex cards, and three forwards from their own
feed checked with the cards.

| Check | What it means | Earned in |
|---|---|---|
| Source behind the verdict | "Is this true?" gets a verdict. Ask "according to what?" and go to the published source. No source: the answer is "no source found," not "false" | Unit 1 |
| Hold: open the link | A reference is a prediction of what references look like. Open it, find the sentence, or treat it as absent | Unit 2 |
| Answer the fear, not the claim | Who is scared, and of what? One action, plain words, no labels, nobody mocked | Unit 3 |
| Read what it read | A screenshot is not a notice. Transcribe the fields it read, then find the real notice on the official channel. The official channel wins | Unit 4 |
| Ends with a person | Anything about the facility itself is answered by a named person or not yet. No invented figure, no patient detail, ever | Unit 5 |

The candidate word is **SHARE**, revealed in 5.4 and named nowhere before it: what the learner
does before they share or reply. Letters are earned in teaching order. Provisional by
construction (build manual §3.3); `kb.framework.share` carries the disclosure rule.

> **The human lines.** The hospital speaks through a named person (5.1,
> `esc.signer-checks-source`). AI never drafts a figure that isn't from a named record, and no
> patient is ever identified, confirmed or denied in a reply. Detection tools are treated as
> unreliable; the check is the source, not a detector. A reply about a person's treatment or a
> dose goes to a clinician, not to the page.

> **Start with the reference lesson:**
> [2.1 A link you haven't opened](cu-2-it-gave-a-source-so-why-wasnt-it-there/scu-2.1-a-link-you-havent-opened/artifact.md)
> is the one sub-unit written out, as a draft, on the Drill way's step flow. Its forward and its
> references are invented and its AI output is expected, not yet reproduced.

## Why this way for this problem

A forward gives you minutes, not an afternoon. The bank review reframed episodic problems as
"checking each new claim as it arrives," which is a reflex, and reflexes are built by repetition
with feedback. A story would teach the check once; a drill runs it eleven times. The way strains
without a clock (five-ways.md §4), so the stations are the first thing to move to Tier 3.

The unit shape: *station, cold run → debrief → station, with the check → reflex card*. Each
unit's tangible is a card; the five together are the deck, run as one full shift in 5.4.

- **1.0** Three minutes. A forward says a kitchen remedy prevents a seasonal fever. The usual
  move: ask the tool "is this true?" It gives a balanced verdict, in full. The learner shares a
  "partly true." The answer sheet asks one question: where did the verdict come from?
- **1.1** Nowhere. The tool predicts what a balanced answer sounds like. The check: ask
  "according to what?" and go to the published source; if there is none, say "no source found."
  *Picture:* "my cousin says."
- **1.2** Two more forwards, three minutes each, with the check. Scored: source named, source
  found, verdict matches source.
- **1.3** Card: *"Where is that written?"*

*Trap from Unit 1:* Sunil now asks for sources, so every verdict comes with references.

- **2.0** Three minutes. A claim arrives with three references: a WHO page, a journal article, a
  ministry notice. The usual move: references means checked. The answer sheet: one doesn't
  exist, one exists and says the opposite, one is real and supports the claim.
- **2.1** A reference is a prediction of what references look like. The check: open it; find the
  sentence; if you can't, the reference is absent and the claim stands unsupported. *Picture:* a
  receipt with a stamp; you ring the number on the stamp.
- **2.2** Two more reference lists, with the check. Scored: each reference opened, the sentence
  found or the reference struck.
- **2.3** Card: *"Open it."*

*Trap from Unit 3:* the replies work, so people send Sunil things to verify.

- **4.0** Three minutes. A screenshot of a notice: the vaccination camp on the 15th is cancelled.
  Letterhead, number, signature. The usual move: ask the tool "is this notice genuine?" It reads
  the text and says it looks genuine. The answer sheet: the real notice on the official channel,
  with a different date. The camp is on.
- **4.1** The tool reads pixels into text and predicts; it has no way to check whether a notice
  was issued. Detection tools don't either, reliably. The check: transcribe the fields it read
  (number, date, signatory), then find the real notice on the official channel. The official
  channel wins. *Picture:* a photocopy of a stamp.
- **4.2** Two more screenshots, with the check. Scored: fields transcribed, official channel
  found, verdict from the channel not the image.
- **4.3** Card: *"What did it read?"*

## Before any more lessons are written

1. **Reproduce the five failures** on free ChatGPT and free Gemini: a verdict with no source
   (1.0), an invented or wrong reference (2.0), a long labelling rebuttal when asked for a reply
   (3.0), "looks genuine" from a screenshot (4.0), invented figures in a drafted denial (5.0). A
   unit whose failure doesn't reproduce is rebuilt, not staged.
2. **Check first:** the bank review found no Nepali health-specific evidence for this problem.
   Talk to two or three people who answer the public for a facility before any more lessons are
   written.
3. **Reviews:** a hospital information officer or health office communication focal person on
   the forwards and the Saturday; a clinician on every claim used, so that no station reproduces
   a real false claim as text people might copy; the programme leads on the Drill way and the
   Tier 3 stations.
4. **Skills:** none assigned. Assign them only from a skill mapper row for this course.

World, practice material and the candidate word are in `_system/graph/knowledge.yaml` as
`kb.world.bheri-corridor`, `kb.rules.practice-forwards` and `kb.framework.share`. Every forward,
post, reference and notice in a lesson is invented; no real remedy or figure is reproduced as
fact.
