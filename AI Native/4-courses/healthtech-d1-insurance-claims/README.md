# Health-tech — Insurance claims and rejections

*Sector course · problem bank **D1** · Course capability: checking insurance claims against the
rules with AI · Framework: none yet · 21 sub-units · **Status: skeleton, draft***

**Bikash entering:** he pastes a claim file into a free AI tool, gets a confident verdict in
seconds, and has started trusting it. **Bikash leaving:** every check he runs is against a written
checklist and the current rules, every flag points to its clause, every field read from paper is
confirmed, and no claim is shaped to look like something it isn't.

**Learner's complaint:** *"It said everything was in order. So why did the claim come back?"*

Bikash is the insurance desk officer at a 100-bed private hospital in Nepalgunj, the one
colleagues bring tangled claim files to. The course follows five failures, each with a different
cause: a check that says "complete" and isn't, a rule the AI made up, a rule that was right last
year, a handwritten date read wrong from a phone photo, and a helpful suggestion that would make
the claim dishonest. Each unit opens on a trap the previous unit's success set.

**Learners:** insurance desk staff, medical recorders, and hospital finance teams.

**What the learner leaves with:** their own written checklist for one kind of claim they handle,
and a one-page claim-check sheet: check against the list, cite the clause, state the rule-set
date, confirm what was read, keep the claim honest.

| Check | What it means | Earned in |
|---|---|---|
| Written checklist | Check against a list, and get a status for every item. Silence isn't "all clear" | Unit 1 |
| Cite the clause | Every flag points to the exact words of a rule supplied. A flag without a clause is a guess | Unit 2 |
| Current rule set | One dated rule set per check, in a fresh chat | Unit 3 |
| Confirm what it read | Check the fields it read from paper before trusting what it concluded | Unit 4 |
| Honest claim, signed | The claim says what happened, and a named person signs it | Unit 5 |

No acronym is proposed. Build manual §3.3 requires the letters to be earned from the failures, not
fitted to a word. If one comes out of the build, it is revealed in 5.3 and named nowhere before it.

> **The human lines.** A person decides every claim: AI points to possible gaps and never passes,
> fails, approves, rejects or scores (`esc.bright-line`, referenced from 5.2). Claims are honest:
> AI never helps a claim look like something it isn't. The signer checks the source: Bikash signs
> what he submits.

## Before any more lessons are written

From the outline, [`5-roadmap/sector-courses/healthtech-d1-insurance-claims.md`](../../5-roadmap/sector-courses/healthtech-d1-insurance-claims.md),
which stays the home of the design reasoning and the open questions:

1. **Reproduce the five failures** on free ChatGPT and free Gemini (outline, "Test before
   writing"). Each unit opens on one of them. A unit whose failure doesn't reproduce is rebuilt,
   not staged. Unit 5 is the likeliest not to reproduce.
2. **Reviews:** someone who works a hospital insurance desk (file contents, checklist, rejection
   reasons); someone who knows the published package rules (every rule, rate and requirement);
   the programme leads (D1 over D2, the cast, the `health` domain).
3. **Skills:** none assigned. Assign them only from a skill mapper row for this course.

Cast, setting and rules are in `_system/graph/knowledge.yaml` as `kb.world.nepalgunj-claims-desk`
and `kb.rules.practice-claim-rules`, both marked placeholder. Every claim file, patient and rule
in a lesson is invented.
