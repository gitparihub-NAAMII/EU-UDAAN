# Problem statements by type of work

Seven problem statements for the 11 sectors in the problem bank, plus a review of all 65
problems in the bank.

**Status:** draft for discussion, 2026-11-13. Not yet reviewed by the programme leads or
tested with anyone working in these sectors.

---

## The approach

The problem bank lists problems by sector: 65 of them across 11 sectors. Reading them side
by side, the same kinds of work keep appearing in different sectors. A health post's
monthly HMIS form, a municipality's progress report and a ministry's NPC report are
different topics but **the same kind of work**: one output, the same reader, new contents
every cycle.

So the problem statements here are written by **type of work**, not by sector:

- **One statement covers many sectors.** A teacher and a section officer can sit in the same
  group, because both are trying to get a draft past a reviewer.
- **Each learner brings their own example.** The statement describes the type; the learner
  chooses one real task from their own job that fits it. The problem bank lists the likely
  examples for each sector.
- **The sector list is a starting point, not a cage.** If a learner's task fits a different
  type better, they move.

An earlier Tangible programme grouped learners this way, and it is used here as a reference
for the method only. No content, names or figures are taken from it.

### The six tests

Every problem in the bank was checked against six tests. A problem that fails one can
often be fixed by narrowing it; a problem that fails several is not a course problem.

| # | Test | The question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Comes back** | Does this task recur daily, weekly or monthly? | The learner practises on live work between sessions, not on an exercise |
| 2 | **Someone is waiting** | Does a real person expect the output? | Work that is owed gets done; optional practice gets skipped |
| 3 | **Has a type** | Does it match one of the seven types below? | The type is what lets learners from different sectors learn from each other |
| 4 | **Has a bar to clear** | Is there a standard or reviewer it must satisfy? | Without one, a draft that reads well but is wrong goes unnoticed |
| 5 | **Safe to practise** | Can it be done with invented or public material? | No real patient, customer, citizen or student data in any AI tool |
| 6 | **A human line** | Can we name the decision that stays with a person? | Every statement must say what AI never decides |

## The seven types at a glance

| # | Type of work | In one line | Sectors where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Same output, new contents** | The report or post that goes out every cycle in the same shape | Health, Health-tech, Agri-tech, Municipality, Small business, Government, Science |

Type 7 is specific to this programme: the bank shows that much of the public-facing work in
Nepal is turning official or technical text into plain Nepali or a local language that
people can act on.

## 1 · Same output, new contents

### What the work looks like

A report, summary or post goes out every day, week or month. The reader and the structure
stay the same; only the contents change. Most of the time goes on gathering inputs, filling
the template and fixing the layout. Very little goes on the part that matters: saying what
changed and why.

### The challenge

**How could this cycle's output take less assembling, so your time goes on what is new?**

Pick one recurring output from your own work. Start with the part that is the same every
time.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Health | H2 · HMIS and DHIS2 monthly reporting |
| Health-tech | D4 · Health data cleaning and dashboards |
| Agri-tech | A5 · Market prices and buyer messages · A6 · Donor and programme reporting |
| Municipality | M4 · Municipal profiles and progress reports |
| Small business | B1 · Social media marketing without a designer |
| Government | G5 · Progress reports for ministries and NPC |
| Science | S2 · DPRs, BOQs and cost estimates |

### What you can safely work with

A past report already made public, or the same layout filled with invented figures.

### What stays with you

Every figure is checked against its source; AI can add up wrongly and can describe a
trend that is not in the data. The person who signs the report is responsible for it.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**H2 · HMIS monthly reporting.** A fixed duty at every facility; 74% on-time reporting, but
only about 38% of facilities report online themselves, and the data is often incomplete.

## 2 · Spot the few that matter

### What the work looks like

Items arrive in a batch: photos, records, rows of data, claims circulating online. Most need
nothing. A few need attention, and someone has to find them, say why each one matters, and
pass them on. The sorting takes the time; the judgement is a small part of it.

### The challenge

**How could the items that need you rise to the top, each with its reason, while you keep
the final call?**

Pick one batch you go through regularly. Start with how you decide that an item needs
attention.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Agri-tech | A2 · Crop and livestock photo triage |
| Health-tech | D4 · Health data cleaning and dashboards (finding bad records) |
| Policy | P5 · Deepfakes and misinformation (with a change, see review) |
| Science | S3 · Data analysis without statistics support (with a change, see review) |

### What you can safely work with

Invented records with a few planted problems, or public photos and posts.

### What stays with you

AI flags; it does not decide. A flagged item is a question for a person, and an item it
did not flag is not proof that nothing is wrong. Outbreaks, takedowns and anything
safety-related need a person.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**A2 · Photo triage.** Research centres report years of technician vacancies, and farmers
say no one is available to advise on pests and disease.

**Note:** this is the thinnest type in the bank. Four problems, two of which need a change.
Consider merging it into type 6 if no stronger examples appear.

## 3 · The draft that has to pass

### What the work looks like

Notes, sources and earlier versions have to become a document that someone will accept: a
reviewer, a committee, a funder, a senior officer. The first draft is rarely the problem.
The problem is the rework: wrong register, a claim with no source, a reviewer's preference
nobody wrote down.

### The challenge

**How could you get from your notes to a draft your reviewer accepts with less rework,
with every claim traceable to its source?**

Pick one document type you write regularly. Start with what your reviewer always sends
back.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Health | H5 · Referral notes, not triage decisions |
| Health-tech | D2 · Electronic records and clinical notes · D3 · Telemedicine case summaries |
| Education | E1 · CDC-aligned lesson plans |
| Fintech | F3 · AML and KYC case narratives (with a change, see review) |
| Small business | B5 · Business plans and loan applications (with a change, see review) |
| Government | G1 · Tippani and formal Nepali drafting |
| Policy | P1 · Reading bills and budgets · P2 · Comparative policy briefs |
| Science | S1 · Literature reviews and academic English · S4 · IEE, EIA and forest clearance documents |

### What you can safely work with

A past, non-confidential document, or an invented one in the same structure. Published
sources: the CDC curriculum, a bill, open-access papers.

### What stays with you

Every fact, figure, reference and commitment is traced to a source before it goes out. AI
can invent a reference or a clause that looks real. The signer is responsible for the
document.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**G1 · Tippani drafting.** The core daily task of the civil service, in a register that AI
often gets wrong. The Auditor General found Rs36.28 billion in spending without supporting
documents.

**Note:** this is the largest type: 11 problems across 8 sectors. If types are taught as
groups, it may need splitting, for example into documents for an official reviewer (G1,
H5, D2, D3) and documents built from sources (P1, P2, S1, S4).

## 4 · The question that keeps coming back

### What the work looks like

Colleagues, customers or farmers ask the same questions every day. Is this message real?
What is the current rule? How do I do this step? The answers live in one person's memory,
an old circular or a chat thread. Each answer takes a few minutes; together they take hours,
and the person asking waits.

### The challenge

**How could people get a reliable answer to a common question without it coming to you
every time?**

Pick the questions you are asked most often. Start with a week's list of them.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Health | H3 · Keeping up with protocols and circulars (with a change, see review) |
| Fintech | F1 · Support queries in Romanised Nepali · F2 · Spotting and explaining scams · F4 · Tracking NRB directives and circulars |
| Agri-tech | A1 · Farmer advisory in Nepali voice and text |
| Municipality | M6 · Explaining building permits to residents |
| Small business | B3 · Customer messages and fraud attempts |
| Government | G2 · Preeti records and staff handovers (with a change, see review) |
| Hospitality | T4 · Permits, routes and altitude information · T5 · Staff turnover and retraining |

### What you can safely work with

Questions written in the style of real ones, and published sources: official circulars,
advisories, permit rules.

### What stays with you

An answer is only as current as the document behind it. AI works from what it is given and
from older patterns, not from today's rule, so every answer must point to its source.
Dose advice, account actions and anything about safety stay with a person.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**F2 · Spotting and explaining scams.** 20,526 cybercrime complaints in FY2025/26, with
wallet hacking and OTP phishing the top fraud types. Every learner is also a potential
victim.

## 5 · Everything still open

### What the work looks like

Many items are open at once: complaints, requests, reviews. Each has an owner, a deadline
and a next step. That information sits across a register, a phone, Facebook messages and
memory. Items slip when the person is busy, and deadlines are now enforced.

### The challenge

**How could every open item and its next step stay visible, so nothing passes its deadline
unnoticed?**

Pick one stream of incoming items from your own work. Start with today's open list.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Municipality | M3 · Grievance triage |
| Government | G4 · Citizen questions and RTI responses |
| Hospitality | T1 · Multilingual reviews |

### What you can safely work with

Invented items with invented names, places and dates.

### What stays with you

Send nothing without reading it. Check that no urgent item has been filed as routine.
Urgency, escalation and the reply itself are decided by a person. Never expose who
complained.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**M3 · Grievance triage.** Hello Sarkar complaints rose 321% to 69,724 in FY2025/26, and
fixed response times are now required.

## 6 · Check it against the rules

### What the work looks like

A file or piece of work has to be checked against a standard: package rules, a rubric, an
act, a building code. Most of it is fine. The work is reading all of it to find the gaps
and writing down each one with its reason.

### The challenge

**How could a first check show every gap and its reason, without a silent result being
taken as "all clear"?**

Pick one kind of file you check regularly. Start with your own checklist, written down.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Health-tech | D1 · Insurance claims and rejections |
| Education | E2 · Feedback for mixed-ability classes (student work against a rubric) · E6 · Remedial plans for non-graded SEE students (merged into E2) |
| Small business | B6 · Fake payment screenshots (with a change, see review) |
| Government | G3 · Procurement documents |
| Hospitality | T7 · Rescue and insurance fraud red flags (with a change, see review) |
| Science | S5 · Checking against the Building Code |

### What you can safely work with

An invented file with planted gaps, checked against a rule set you supply yourself.

### What stays with you

AI points to possible gaps; it does not pass, fail, mark, score or approve. A check that
misses a gap and reports "complete" is worse than no check. Claims must be honest: AI never
helps inflate or fake one.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**D1 · Insurance claims.** Unpaid claims owed to hospitals were reported at Rs10 billion to
over Rs16 billion during 2026, about one claim in five has been reported rejected, and major
hospitals suspended insurance services.

## 7 · Official words into plain words

### What the work looks like

An official notice, rule, prescription or warning has to reach people who will act on it:
patients, parents, farmers, savers, guests. The source is in formal or technical language,
sometimes in English. The audience needs plain Nepali or a local language, often by SMS or
voice. One wrong word, a dose or a date, can cause harm.

### The challenge

**How could official information reach the people who need it in words they can act on,
without losing anything that matters?**

Pick one kind of notice or instruction you pass on regularly. Start with a recent one and
the people it was for.

### Where it shows up

| Sector | Problems from the bank |
|---|---|
| Health | H1 · Plain-Nepali patient instructions |
| Education | E5 · School admin and parent notices |
| Fintech | F5 · Cooperative and remittance literacy · F6 · E-Commerce Act and QR onboarding (with a change, see review) |
| Agri-tech | A3 · Crop and livestock insurance explained · A4 · Fertiliser and seed supply updates |
| Municipality | M5 · Disaster warnings in local languages (with a change, see review) |
| Small business | B2 · Digital records and tax rules |
| Hospitality | T2 · Language barriers with guests · T3 · Crisis communication to guests (both with a change, see review) |

### What you can safely work with

Published notices, advisories and acts, and invented prescriptions or messages. No
patient, student or customer details.

### What stays with you

Every plain version is checked against the official source, word by word where doses,
dates, amounts or safety are involved. Translations into Maithili, Tharu and other local
languages need a fluent person to check them. The official source wins any disagreement.

### Strongest example for the meeting

**H1 · Plain-Nepali patient instructions.** Most primary health care centres run with one
doctor, and female literacy is 69.4% (Census 2021). It shows the core habit early: check
the AI version where one wrong dose word is dangerous.

### What the review shows

- **Most of the bank holds up.** 52 of 65 problems fit as written or with a stated change.
- **Some items are topics, not tasks.** D6 (privacy), P4 (regulating AI) and E3 (students'
  AI use) are important, but nobody does them as weekly work. They belong in course content
  or in the "What stays with you" line of every statement.
- **Episodic problems need reframing, not dropping.** Disasters, crises and deepfake waves
  (M5, T3, P5) cannot be counted on to happen during a course. Preparing checked templates
  in advance turns them into practice work.
- **Overlaps are common.** H2 and D4, H5 and D3, E2 and E6, B3 and B6, F6 and B2, T4 and T7,
  M3 and G4, M6 and S5. Grouping by type puts overlapping problems in the same room instead
  of building them twice.
- **Type 7 is the programme's own.** Turning official words into plain Nepali or a local
  language appears in 7 of 11 sectors.

## Open questions for the meeting

- **Seven types or fewer.** Type 2 is thin (four problems). Merge it into type 6, or keep it
  and look for stronger examples?
- **Type 3 is large.** Eleven problems across eight sectors. Split it in two?
- **Learner fit for Policy.** MPs or their staff?
- **Validation.** The five "Check first" items and T1 (Medium evidence) need a practitioner
  conversation before anything is built.
- **Evidence currency.** All figures are from the problem bank as written. Several are 2026
  news reports and can change quickly.
