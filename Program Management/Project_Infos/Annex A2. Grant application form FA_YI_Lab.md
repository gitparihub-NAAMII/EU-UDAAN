## 2 The action

### 2.1 Description of the action

#### 2.1.1 Description

##### (a) Relevance and context analysis

Nepal has a significant demographic advantage, with over 40% of its population under 40. However, youth unemployment remains high at 20.54% (Census, 2021), and more than 2,000 young people migrate abroad daily due to limited job opportunities, inadequate access to quality education, and persistent socio-political and economic barriers. While digital transformation is viewed as a pathway to progress, its benefits remain uneven: urban centers like Kathmandu have advanced digital infrastructure, but rural and marginalized communities face poor internet access, low digital literacy, and limited pathways to digital employment.

Despite national frameworks like the ICT Policy (2015) and the Digital Nepal Framework (2019), local-level implementation remains weak and often fails to address the needs of youth, especially women, indigenous, and marginalized groups. A 2025 YI-Lab assessment found that youth cite a lack of job opportunities (40%), professional networks (23%), and digital skills (16%) as key barriers. The same study highlighted that CSOs face significant capacity gaps in cybersecurity, digital rights, and content creation. These challenges intersect across three core areas: access to digital tools and finance; individual and organizational capacity development; and opportunities for mentorship, innovation, and market access. The current ecosystem lacks the infrastructure, coordination, and localized support to prepare youth and civil society for meaningful participation in the digital economy.

In direct response, the proposed action, **Unleashing Digital Acceleration and Ambition of Nepali Youth (UDAAN)**, is a 48-month initiative designed to increase the inclusion of young Nepali women and men in the digital world and job market by advancing digital literacy, upskilling innovation, and future market readiness. UDAAN directly addresses the specific objective of the EuropeAid/183733/DD/ACT/NP call (Lot 3 – Youth in Digital) and all three call priorities: (1) empowering underrepresented youth and women, (2) fostering entrepreneurship and job creation, and (3) strengthening local innovation ecosystems through grassroots initiatives.

Aligned with the global objective of strengthening the EU's partnership with civil society actors in Nepal, UDAAN is led by a consortium of **Youth Innovation Lab (YI-Lab)**, **Heifer Project Nepal (HPN)**, **Nepal Applied Mathematics and Informatics Institute for Research (NAAMII)**, and **Women in Animation Nepal (WIA-Nepal)**. It is structured around four interlinked action components:

- **Pathshala** (a place of learning): equips youth with market-relevant digital skills.
- **Karyashala** (a place of work for innovation and experimentation): dedicated spaces for innovation and creative experimentation.
- **Karmashala** (a workplace in a professional setting): fosters entrepreneurship and job creation through public-private collaboration.
- **Nitishala** (a place for setting policies and guidance): strengthens municipal systems and policies to institutionalize innovation and digital governance.

Together, these contribute to the EU call's expected results: increased youth employability, inclusive digital participation, and youth-led innovation.

The action mainstreams cross-cutting issues with a gender-transformative focus to ensure equitable digital participation of young Nepali women and community-based organizations (CBOs), especially those from marginalized, minority, and remote communities. It aligns with the EU Gender Action Plan III (Principle 4: equal participation and leadership; Principle 6: opportunities in the green and digital transitions) and the EU Action Plan on Human Rights and Democracy.

Grounded in the Human Rights-Based Approach (HRBA), the action applies its five principles: (i) legality, universality, and indivisibility of human rights, (ii) participation, (iii) non-discrimination, (iv) accountability, and (v) transparency. It integrates Gender Equality and Social Inclusion (GESI) principles. Gender equality is central to the design: **WIA-Nepal leads outreach and mentorship in digital and creative industries; HPN anchors rural youth engagement through innovation hubs embedded in municipalities and agri-cooperatives; NAAMII provides technical leadership in AI and emerging technologies; and YI-Lab integrates digital rights, media literacy, and civic-tech applications.**

To ensure synergy and avoid duplication, the action builds on EU-supported initiatives such as Nepali Yuwa in Climate Action and Green Growth (theYouthCAN.org, a four-year EU-supported YI-Lab project) and complements GRAPE-II (agri-tech innovation), UNESCO's Media and Information Literacy frameworks, and UNDP's civic engagement programs. It contributes to the EU Youth Action Plan 2022–2027 and SDG 4, 5, 8, 13 and 16.

**Target groups and final beneficiaries**

The action targets youth aged 16 to 35 (the National Youth Policy-2082 defines youth as 18–35), with a focus on young women and men from marginalized and indigenous communities. Additional target groups: youth organizations, local governments and elected representatives, startups, academic institutions, and CSOs/CBOs focusing on agri-cooperatives. Final beneficiaries: youth and women, including those in rural areas, disadvantaged, ethnic, and minority communities, vulnerable populations, and residents across diverse regions.

**Key stakeholder groups and their attitudes:** Young people, youth organizations, local governments, the private sector, and academic institutions. YI-Lab's April 2025 assessment shows youth seek employment and entrepreneurship but face limited job openings, weak networks and insufficient digital skills; their demand for digital professionalism training shows readiness for Pathshala's tiered schools and Karmashala's internships and fellowships. Local governments are willing to foster innovation hubs and integrate digital literacy into municipal plans but need capacity and skilled personnel (addressed by Nitishala's fellowship model and policy dialogues). The private sector is invested in youth-led innovation and workforce development in AI, e-commerce and geospatial services, and supports Karyashala's hackathons and bootcamps, while emphasizing soft skills and practical experience. Academic institutions are aligning curricula with market demand and are open to co-developing content and hosting innovation activities.

Throughout the project cycle, target groups will be engaged through inclusive forums, digital surveys and feedback mechanisms to shape activities, monitor progress and adapt continuously. Innovation hubs will be participatory platforms for youth-led initiatives and peer-to-peer learning.

**Technical and management capacities of target groups and co-applicants:** Youth have growing digital literacy, active peer networks and familiarity with online platforms. Municipal governments have governance and planning systems and operational authority, making them well-positioned to host and manage innovation hubs. Academic institutions provide computer labs, training halls, libraries and qualified faculty. CSOs and cooperatives offer community mobilization capacity and local trust networks. Startups demonstrate technical ingenuity in agriculture, fintech and e-commerce.

YI-Lab brings a track record of multi-partner civic-tech initiatives and experience with government and youth groups. **NAAMII contributes cutting-edge expertise in AI education and competency-based matching of youth with jobs, internships, and fellowships.** HPN anchors community-level implementation with three decades of engagement with municipalities and cooperatives. WIA provides training and mentorship in animation and creative technologies, fostering women's participation in the digital economy.

##### (b) Intervention logic

**From activities to outputs:** The action follows a structured results chain producing six key outputs. Over 48 months: a six-month preparatory phase (internal capacity building, consortium coordination, onboarding, groundwork); three 12-month implementation cycles covering the four components; and a final six months for closure (evaluations, documentation, stakeholder handover).

###### Output 1.1: Digital skills and knowledge strengthened among young women and men, entrepreneurs, government officials, and CSO/CBOs

IF the AI School, Animation School, and Coding School are launched, AND youth, women, and marginalized groups remain motivated and have reliable access to affordable devices and connectivity, THEN digital skills and knowledge will be strengthened among diverse groups.

Courses build competencies across three tiers: beginner (**native** – learners build an AI-first mindset and implement AI in daily workflows), intermediate (**foundation** – learners build AI systems with industry use cases), and professional (**deep** – learners do deep AI research in different domains). Beginner courses target high school students, early-career professionals, government officials (Activity 2.3.2b), and CSO/CBO representatives. Intermediate courses target youth and professionals entering the digital workforce. Professional courses prepare participants for employment and innovation in national and international digital markets. Special emphasis is placed on women's participation, particularly in animation.

Indicators: (1) number of young women and men completing foundational and deep AI courses; (2) number of women completing intermediate and professional animation courses; (3) number of young women and men completing intermediate and professional coding courses. Disaggregated by sex, age, disability, rural/urban location and ethnic group. Data collected by NAAMII, WIA-Nepal and YI-Lab, plus completion records and analytics from **Tangible Careers** and Shikshya.org; verified with competency-based rubrics, project portfolios and certification records.

#### 2.1.2 Methodology

##### i. Implementation methods and rationale

The action is structured around four interrelated components and integrates a risk-informed approach across all seven provinces:

1. **Pathshala** builds foundational digital competencies through multi-level courses in AI, coding, animation, cybersecurity and digital rights.
2. **Karyashala** channels skills into innovation and entrepreneurship through hackathons and bootcamps.
3. **Karmashala** connects youth to real-world opportunities through internships and fellowships, and links youth to jobs, training and scholarships.
4. **Nitishala** ensures sustainability through institutional support and policy engagement: training and digital literacy for elected representatives and local government, policy dialogues, SOPs for innovation hubs, a podcast series, and integration of content into school curricula.

Each component follows three phases: **preparatory** (curriculum development for the AI, animation and coding schools; agreements with local governments; youth outreach), **implementation** (courses, hackathons, bootcamps, internships, policy dialogues), and **handover** (documenting best practices, institutionalizing hubs, sharing lessons).

- **Hybrid and multi-level approach:** combines Shikshya.org and Tangible Careers with in-person bootcamps, hackathons and fellowships, and structured policy engagement; low-bandwidth online delivery for remote youth plus hands-on in-person experiences.
- **Stakeholder engagement and co-creation:** formal MoUs with selected local governments for hub ownership; cooperatives integrate digital literacy; private sector provides mentorship, internships and market access; open access to beginner and intermediate courses on Shikshya.org; competitive selection for advanced training and fellowships; participatory policy dialogues and curriculum working groups.
- **Capacity development and iterative learning:** hybrid training; fellows use a training-of-trainers approach; winners receive mentorship and incubation; content localized to Nepal and, where appropriate, to provinces; short, repeated sessions with practice and feedback.

**Core inputs**
- **Equipment & software:** high-performance computers, licensed software, projectors and reliable internet to simulate professional environments; dedicated systems for the Animation School; high-speed internet and AV for blended classes; physically accessible venues.
- **Learning materials:** curricula, modules, assignments, project briefs, SOP manuals, digital literacy toolkits and policy briefs for the schools, fellowships and internships, and municipal hubs.
- **Human resources:** trainers, mentors, fellowship supervisors, career coaches and programme staff with expertise in AI, animation, coding, digital rights and entrepreneurship.
- **Digital platforms:** Shikshya.org upgraded to a mobile app with low-bandwidth streaming, interactive modules and gamification; **Tangible Careers improved with offline synchronisation, mobile optimisation, Nepal-specific datasets and culturally adapted interfaces.**

**Rationale:** the four components form a pipeline from foundational digital skills to practical innovation, professional experience and policy engagement; the hybrid model keeps learning accessible in low-connectivity areas while maintaining industry-standard in-person training; co-creation keeps interventions locally owned and demand-driven; iterative training and mentorship turn learning into employment and entrepreneurship.

##### ii. Continuation of previous action

Not a direct continuation, but built on lessons and proven models. **The Tangible Careers platform, developed by NAAMII, has pioneered a competency-based model that bridges the gap between learning and hiring by mapping skills against industry standards and enabling mentor-led evaluation.** Under UDAAN it will gain offline synchronization and mobile optimization and link youth directly to internships and fellowships. Shikshya.org (YI-Lab) has reached thousands of learners and will become a mobile app. The design draws on theYouthCAN.org's Innovation Fellowship model and bootcamps (value of embedding fellows in local government, mentorship for enterprises, and strong municipal ownership). The AI, Animation and Coding Schools build on the expertise of NAAMII, WIA-Nepal and YI-Lab; HPN's experience anchors innovation within community structures.

##### iii. Potential synergies with other initiatives

Complements the Digital Nepal Framework (2019), Startup Policy (2024), Youth Vision 2025, the 16th Periodic Plan and the National Cybersecurity Policy (2023). Builds on theYouthCAN.org and complements UNESCO and EU-supported Media and Information Literacy programs, GIZ's GRAPE II, World Bank digital innovation initiatives and private sector incubators.

#### 2.1.4 Sustainability of the action

##### i. Expected long-lasting benefits

- **Technical:** more than **40,000** young women and men acquire market-relevant competencies in AI, coding, animation, cybersecurity and digital rights; Pathshala's tiered training continues after the project, offering digital education and job-matching; Municipal Innovation Labs in **14** municipalities remain as hubs.
- **Economic:** more than **420** youth-led enterprises supported through hackathons, bootcamps and incubation; over **800** internships and fellowships; **30%** of participants expected to secure permanent employment or start businesses during the project; hubs and the Creator's Carnival keep attracting investment and mentorship.
- **Social:** at least **40%** of participants women; alumni networks of fellows, interns and innovators; municipal labs support rural youth and cooperatives.
- **Policy and institutional:** hubs institutionalized within municipal structures with SOPs; digital literacy and emerging technologies integrated into local curricula.

##### ii. Detailed risk analysis and contingency plan

| Proposed action | Identified risk | Contingency plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Pathshala** – digital skills (AI, coding, animation, cybersecurity, digital rights) | **Physical:** limited ICT infrastructure (poor internet, outages). **Environmental:** monsoon, floods, landslides delay in-person training. **Social:** gender and social barriers limit participation of women, indigenous and marginalized youth. | Upgrade Shikshya.org with low-bandwidth, offline and mobile features; distribute offline training kits (USBs, manuals); flexible scheduling and moving sessions online; targeted outreach to ensure at least 40% women. |
| **Karyashala** – innovation and entrepreneurship (hackathons, bootcamps, incubation) | **Physical:** limited incubation space. **Political:** elections and volatility disrupt events. **Economic:** limited sustainable investment for young enterprises. **Social:** rural and marginalized youth face barriers to attending. | Hybrid/virtual events; reschedule or relocate during unrest or disasters; align calendar with election cycles; diversify partnerships and link enterprises with cooperatives, banks and accelerators; travel subsidies and regional events. |
| **Karmashala** – internships, fellowships | **Physical:** distance between mentors and mentees. **Environmental:** weather delays training and fieldwork. **Political:** change in municipal leadership affects fellowship and hubs. **Economic:** limited job absorption by hosts. **Social:** unequal access for marginalized youth. | MoUs with municipalities for continuity; expand host organizations across private sector, academia and cooperatives; targeted outreach in remote areas; digital tools to strengthen mentor–mentee relationships. |
| **Nitishala** – policy, institutionalization and advocacy (hubs, dialogues, curriculum, digital literacy for officials) | **Physical:** no permanent innovation space in some municipalities. **Environmental:** extreme weather delays workshops. **Political:** weak ownership, limited budgets, election instability. **Economic:** municipalities lack resources post-project. **Social:** officials resist youth participation. | Secure space via local colleges, cooperatives, FNCCI and municipal facilities; flexible and hybrid workshops; build ownership through training, advocacy and joint implementation; push for municipal budget lines; link hubs with academic and cooperative networks. |
| **Cross-cutting** – project management, EU visibility, financial oversight | **Political:** unrest, protests, elections. **Economic:** exchange rate fluctuations or delayed disbursements. **Physical/environmental:** pandemics or disasters. **Social/reputational:** non-compliance with EU visibility rules. | Contingency activity calendar, neutrality; quarterly financial reviews, contingency budget lines and consortium reserves; move online during pandemics/disasters, staff safety and insurance; robust EU Communication & Visibility Plan with communications officer, graphic designer and video editor. |

##### iii. Possibilities for multiplier effects

Open-access Shikshya.org remains available after the project; the fellowship model is adaptable across sectors; the Creator's Carnival and Innovation Unplugged podcast showcase innovations; SOPs and policy briefs offer replicable models; strong potential for government uptake, including coding and AI in the national curriculum; community-level IT literacy camps, cooperative digitalization and peer learning; knowledge shared through EU platforms and Heifer International. **The Tangible platform amplifies these effects as Nepal's core digital skills infrastructure, bridging education with industry, startups and e-governance; its adaptability across South Asia, integration with 50+ international research institutions, open-source features and global licensing potential make it a replicable model for AI education worldwide.**

##### iv. Sustainability after completion

- **a. Financial:** seed funding, mentorship and finance links help enterprises become self-sustaining; Municipal Innovation Hubs co-financed through agreements with local governments, with budget lines for equipment, internet and staffing; Shikshya.org courses sustained through low-cost, scalable delivery; cost-effective models documented for independent continuation.
- **b. Institutional:** hubs run under SOPs with trained staff; AI, Coding and Animation Schools remain on Shikshya.org with trained educators; curriculum integration at school and university level; advocacy for institutionalizing the fellowship approach and municipal innovation committees; each partner strengthens its own expertise (YI-Lab civic tech and policy dialogue, HPN community innovation, NAAMII and WIA youth engagement in AI and animation).
- **c. Policy:** youth-responsive, pro-digital municipal policies; alignment with the Digital Nepal Framework, Startup Policy (2024) and Youth Vision 2025, and evidence for revising the National Youth Policy; **the Tangible Careers platform will serve as a foundation for a national digital skills strategy, with competency frameworks aligned to international standards and tailored to Nepal**; policy briefs on scaling hubs, digital literacy for officials, digital rights and GDPR-aligned data protection.
- **d. Environmental:** a Greening Matrix monitored throughout; hackathon and bootcamp challenges on climate-smart agriculture, renewable energy and waste management; hybrid and digital delivery reduces travel and printing.

**Amplify Change (NAAMII)** – Increased youth awareness of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and abortion systems in Nepal using a chatbot and dedicated social media platforms that inform young people about abortion policies and services.

**Data Collection for Third Party Ultrasound Partners (NAAMII)** – Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation project progressing with active collaboration among partners across regions, ensuring rich data collection, shared expertise, and learning and capacity development.
