## How we are approaching it

1. **Problem first, name it after.** Every unit opens with a competent person (Maya, a Kathmandu trip coordinator) hitting a real failure; the concept arrives as the answer. Frameworks are discovered, not announced.
2. **Three courses, one story.** BRIEF (brief the AI well) → AGENT (hand it a job safely) → TRUST (stand behind its output). Each course starts from a problem the previous one's success created.
3. **Free tools, no code, local context.** Nepal's working conditions: monsoon, Tihar, WhatsApp, free AI tools.
4. **Cohorts through partners.** Colleges, municipalities, companies and government bodies supply learners and venues. Partnership pipeline: stakeholder map → cold reach → concept note → focal briefing → survey → cohort preparation → delivery → reporting.
5. **Sector-wise coverage (TAP).** At least one cohort in each of 9 sectors, with learner feedback from every cohort feeding sector-wise modules. Covered so far: Education, Science & engineering, Health, Municipalities. Still to cover: Fintech, Government organisations, Hospitality, Small enterprises, Policymakers/MPs.
6. **Verified skills, not attendance.** Learners claim skills on Tangible Careers and receive multi-partner certificates; course videos go to Shikshya.org.
7. **Beyond the valley.** Online cohorts and the online delivery artifact reach learners outside Kathmandu.

## Why this approach

| Choice | Why |
|---|---|
| AI Native for everyone first | It is the broad base of the AI Skills Pyramid: everyone who works alongside AI needs it before Foundation or Deep. |
| Problem-first teaching | Learners remember a failure they felt; definitions first do not stick, especially for non-technical learners. |
| Short, cohort-based delivery with institutions | 20,000 learners is only reachable at scale; partners bring learners, rooms and follow-up. |
| Nine sectors | Proves relevance across the economy, meets the grant's inclusion aim, and generates the feedback for sector modules. |
| Tangible Careers certification | Evidence of competence for the EU and for employers, and the route into AI Foundation and Karmashala internships. |
| Online and open cohorts | In-person venues limit reach beyond the Kathmandu Valley (a risk named in both monthly reports). |

## Risks and what we are doing

- **Behind the 2026 target** → larger institutional cohorts (ISMT, Uniglobe, Global, MBL Bank, NEA), open and online cohorts, and more trainers (7 shortlisted).
- **Records don't fully match** (September 171 vs 186; MMIHS 42 vs 60; the 19 Sep Jyoti Life Insurance session is on the calendar but in no report) → consolidate attendance, gender-disaggregated and completion records for every cohort.
- **Tangible certificate not finished** → completion after Chhath (fourth week of November); evidence collection continues meanwhile.
- **MoUs depend on partner approvals** → regular follow-up; Budhanilkantha Municipality MoU in progress.
